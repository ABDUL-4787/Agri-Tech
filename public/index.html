1

Spread Fruit

Place 1 to 20 tomatoes separated on a flat, light-coloured surface (newspaper, white cloth, or tray).

2

Place Reference Coin

Place a standard ₹10 or ₹5 coin flat beside the tomatoes at the same height.

Direct Top-Down Photo

Photograph directly from above in clear daylight. Avoid harsh shadows or strong camera tilt.

Avoid Severe Overlap

Ensure fruit do not overlap heavily so individual contours can be measured accurately.

      How AgriGrade Inspects & Measures Tomatoes

      AgriGrade employs transparent, reproducible computer vision algorithms running on standard CPU hardware. Here is how each photograph is processed:

      1. Image Validation & Lighting Check

      Photographs are checked for blur using Laplacian variance (threshold ≥ 65) and lighting intensity. Blurry or extremely dark photographs produce calibration warnings.

      2. Reference Coin Calibration

      The application detects the standard ₹10 (27mm) or ₹5 (23mm) coin using circularity and color thresholding. By dividing coin diameter in pixels by nominal diameter in millimetres, it calculates the exact pixels_per_mm scale.

      3. Watershed Segmentation for Touching Fruit

      Euclidean distance transformation separates tomatoes that touch each other on the table without confusing them as a single large fruit.

      4. Surface Color & Defect Heuristics

      Using HSV and Lab color spaces, AgriGrade calculates the red surface percentage to identify ripening stages (Mature Green to Mature Red) and detects dark blemishes, necrotic spots, or surface cracks.

      5. Important Technical Limitations

      An ordinary external photograph cannot detect internal fruit rot, hollow cavities, or seed gel ripeness. A single coin cannot fully correct severe camera tilt or perspective angle. AgriGrade is an independent benchmark for mandi negotiations, not a certified legal grading system.

      Tomato Grading Standards & Thresholds

      AgriGrade applies deterministic engineering interpretations based on DMI/AGMARK (Directorate of Marketing & Inspection, Government of India) and UNECE FFV-36 standards.

      Class Max Surface Defects Min Axis Ratio Requirements & Tolerances
      Extra Class < 1.0% ≥ 0.90 Superficial defects < 1%, axis ratio ≥ 0.90, uniform color, no cracks/decay.
      Class I ≤ 3.0% ≥ 0.80 Defects ≤ 3%, axis ratio ≥ 0.80, slight shape variation allowed.
      Class II ≤ 8.0% ≥ 0.65 Defects ≤ 8%, axis ratio ≥ 0.65, greenback allowed provided fruit is firm.
      Below Standard > 8.0% < 0.65 Defects > 8%, axis ratio < 0.65, undersized fruit (<35mm round, <30mm oblong), or cracks/decay.

      Lot Tolerances: Extra allows maximum 5% out-of-grade; Class I and Class II allow maximum 10% out-of-grade.

      Field Validation Notice: These thresholds are provisional interpretations for market transparency and dispute resolution. Formal certification requires an authorized AGMARK grading inspector.
